  • Tiered Rates (7.0% & 9.9%): 7.0% applies to taxable LTCG up to $1,000,000 (after the CPI-indexed standard deduction); 9.9% applies to taxable LTCG exceeding $1,000,000 (e.g. from taxable brokerage sales and CRUT/CRAT Tier 2 gains).

3. Federal Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)

IRC § 1411 3.8% Medicare Surtax Engine

Affects both progressive Federal Ordinary/LTCG tax brackets and the unindexed NIIT MAGI threshold

NIIT Exposure Formula (3.8% Surtax):

Applies 3.8% to the lesser of:

  1. Net Investment Income (NII): Taxable brokerage dividends & realized capital gains + CRUT/CRAT Tier 1 (ordinary) & Tier 2 (capital gains) distributions.
  2. MAGI Excess: Total Modified Adjusted Gross Income (including earned income, Pre-Tax draws, RMDs, Roth conversions, and NII) minus the statutory threshold ($200k Single / $250k MFJ).

Key Insights from Gottlieb (2026) Framework:
  • Hold-and-Draw is the Toughest Benchmark: A CRUT frequently beats an immediate taxable sale (Track C) due to avoiding Day-1 capital gains drag, but beating a buy-and-hold strategy (Track B) depends heavily on asset cost basis, state tax overlays, and living beyond the break-even horizon.
  • Payout Rate Optimization: Defaulting to the statutory minimum 5% payout often reduces CRUT win probability by ~6% compared to higher, statutorily permissible payout rates (e.g. 6%–8%), as higher payouts return cash flow to the client more efficiently.
  • The § 7520 Rate Paradox: While the § 7520 discount rate dictates the upfront charitable deduction value, varying it from 1.2% to 8.2% changes the ongoing win probability by less than 5 percentage points. Ongoing performance is governed primarily by embedded gain, turnover, and longevity.
